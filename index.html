1. What Changes in the Part 107 Test on October 26, 2026?

Beginning October 26, 2026, the FAA transitions Part 107 sectional chart questions away from static figures in the Airman Knowledge Testing Supplement (FAA-CT-8080-2H) toward current VFR sectional chart excerpts.

  • What stays identical: All 14 CFR Part 107 operating rules, airspace class rules (Class B, C, D, E, G), obstacle height labels (MSL bold above vs. (AGL) in parentheses), and airport communication symbols (circled C for CTAF, UNICOM, ASOS/AWOS).
  • How to study: Practice reading real chart symbols on dated VFR sectional chart excerpts rather than memorizing static figure numbers. Try both formats free on Part 107 HQ Sectional Chart Practice and review the October 2026 Sectional Chart Changes Guide.

3. Worked Sectional Chart Diagrams

AGL vs. MSL & Structure 400-Foot Rule

Airport elevation (606 ft) and top obstacle figures are in feet MSL; numbers in parentheses like (325) are feet AGL. Under 14 CFR §107.51(b), flying within 400 ft of a lighted tower allows up to 400 ft above the structure's top.

Class E Floors: Surface vs. 700 ft & 1,200 ft AGL

A dashed magenta line means Class E starts at the surface (SFC) and requires FAA authorization (14 CFR §107.41). A shaded magenta vignette means Class E begins at 700 ft AGL (with Class G below).

CTAF Circled-C vs. UNICOM & ASOS

A circled C identifies the Common Traffic Advisory Frequency (CTAF) for self-announcing position at nontowered airports. Separate weather broadcast frequencies (ASOS/AWOS) from CTAF and UNICOM.

Airport Traffic Patterns & Right-of-Way

Standard traffic patterns use left turns unless marked RP. Remote pilots must yield right-of-way to all manned aircraft (14 CFR §107.37) and avoid interfering with airport operations (14 CFR §107.43).
